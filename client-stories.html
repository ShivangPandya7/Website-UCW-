Business exitWealth Advisory and Asset Management

Turning a liquidity event into a durable plan.

A second-generation promoter family in Gujarat sold a controlling stake in their manufacturing business after three decades of building it. The proceeds — a number that felt abstract until it landed in an account — needed to become something durable, not merely liquid.

Rather than deploying the full amount at once, we staged capital into UCWF over eight weeks, paired it with a bond ladder for near-term liquidity, and set up a structured insurance review for the family’s changed risk profile. Eighteen months later, the family has a single advisor across all three moving parts, not three separate relationships to coordinate themselves.

SuccessionFamily office

Bringing the next generation into the room.

A multi-generation trading family had always made investment decisions informally: the patriarch made the calls, and the rest of the family found out afterwards. As the second generation took on more responsibility, that stopped working.

We restructured quarterly reviews to include both generations, built reporting that made the portfolio’s logic legible rather than just its numbers, and introduced a documented succession framework alongside the family’s legal advisors. The goal was not to change the investment approach. It was to make sure it could survive a handover.

NRI investingWealth Advisory and Insurance

One relationship, instead of five scattered ones.

An NRI professional in the UAE had India holdings spread across four distributors, an overseas brokerage account, and no coordinated estate plan. Nobody had the full picture — including him.

We consolidated reporting into a single view, rationalised his mutual fund holdings from eleven schemes to a coherent five-fund allocation, and worked alongside his lawyer on a cross-border estate structure. Reviews now happen over video, on his schedule, with everything visible in one place.

Wealth preservationAsset Management

Staying invested when it was hardest to.

During a sharp correction, a long-standing UCGF client called wanting to exit entirely. Rather than simply processing the redemption, we walked through the fund’s own downside framework with her: what the mandate was designed to do in exactly this scenario, and what selling now would lock in.

She stayed invested. The portfolio recovered over the following two quarters. The call did not change the fund’s process — it is what the process is for.
